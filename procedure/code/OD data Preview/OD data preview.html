Introduction

This notebook documents my learning process while exploring the spanishoddata package and the Spanish Origin-Destination (OD) mobility dataset. The objective is to understand how the data are structured and how they can be used in my thesis on fine-scale population flow simulation for infectious disease modelling.

Understanding the OD Dataset

Each row represents an aggregated flow of trips, not an individual person.

Example:

Variable Meaning
date Date of travel
hour Hour of travel
id_origin Origin district
id_destination Destination district
distance Distance category
activity_origin Activity at origin
activity_destination Activity at destination
age Age group
sex Sex
income Income category
n_trips Number of trips
trips_total_length_km Total distance travelled

What Does One Row Mean?

Suppose one row contains

  • Date = 2024-03-01
  • Hour = 08
  • Origin = District A
  • Destination = District B
  • Age = 25–39
  • Trips = 148

Interpretation:

Approximately 148 trips were made between District A and District B during that hour by people in the specified demographic group.

Important Notes

This dataset does not contain:

  • Individual GPS trajectories
  • Personal identifiers
  • Exact travel routes

Instead, it contains aggregated Origin-Destination flows.

Why Is This Useful?

The OD dataset provides coarse movement patterns between districts.

For my thesis, these coarse flows will eventually be:

Coarse OD
        ↓
Downscaling
        ↓
Fine-scale OD
        ↓
Agent-Based Model
        ↓
Disease Transmission Simulation
